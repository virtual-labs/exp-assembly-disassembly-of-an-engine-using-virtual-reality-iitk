### About the Experiment 

The actual lab pain point in hands-on mechanical training involves logistics, safety, and
component health. Engines are heavy, dirty, and pose a risk of cuts or pinched fingers during
disassembly. Students often struggle to visualize how internal parts (like the connecting rod or
crankshaft) interact when the casing is opaque. Furthermore, sharing a limited number of
damaged or worn-out lab engines compromises the learning experience, and the instructor
must constantly monitor students to prevent damage to expensive tools and internal engine
components due to incorrect force or procedure.<br>
The VR innovation delivers a clean, accessible, and pedagogically rich simulation. The VR
environment allows for risk-free manipulation of components without grease, heavy lifting, or
physical injury. The system provides guided assembly and real-time feedback, ensuring the
correct sequential steps and proper tool use. Crucially, the virtual engine can be dissected or
made transparent to display the complete cycle of combustion and mechanics, offering an
unparalleled understanding of function and spatial relationships that no real-world, cutaway model can fully replicate.
